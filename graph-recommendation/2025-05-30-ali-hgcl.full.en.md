# Recommendation papers (2025.5.31): Alibaba shows that similar items improve recommendation

> [中文版](2025-05-30-ali-hgcl.md)

1 HGCL: Hierarchical Graph Contrastive Learning for User-Item Recommendation

Link: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.19020

Company: Alibaba (Amap / Gaode)

Area: GNN representation learning

Overview:

Applies the idea of clustering to GNN representation learning to make representations more accurate. Items are clustered, so a user–item interaction can be turned into an interaction between the user and the item's cluster, and this relation is added into the GNN modeling. Specifically:

(1) Stage-wise construction

The model is built in 3 stages, in order. First, cross-layer contrastive learning (based on XSimGCL) produces initial user and item representations. Then item nodes are clustered to build a two-level user–item bipartite graph. Finally, representations are jointly optimized on the original user–item graph and the user–item-cluster graph to produce the final recommendations.

(2) Clustering method

Item embeddings are first reduced in dimension with t-SNE, then clustered.

(3) Joint training

At the end, three tasks are trained jointly: recommendation on the original user–item graph, recommendation on the user–item-cluster graph, and cross-layer contrast on the original user–item graph.

Takeaways:

(1) As the authors say in the introduction, item clustering both effectively eases item sparsity and broadens the range of user interactions. Long-tail and new items with few interactions get generalization; a user who interacts with sports video items can be lifted to interacting with general entertainment videos.

(2) There is room for improvement. The paper clusters items; users could be clustered as well, which would address user sparsity and also broaden the range of item interactions. There is also room elsewhere, for example hierarchical clustering.

(3) The clustering method in this paper is quite interesting and worth noting. Item node representations are reduced to 2D with t-SNE, then split by radius (ρ) and angle (θ); item nodes falling in the same sector form one cluster, for ρ*θ clusters in total.

Credibility: offline

Recommendation: worth a look
