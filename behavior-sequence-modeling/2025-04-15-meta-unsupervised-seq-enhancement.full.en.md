Recommendation System in Advertising and Streaming Media: Unsupervised Data Enhancement Sequence Suggestions

Link: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.08740

Company: Meta

Area: sequence modeling

Overview:

To address data sparsity and noise in sequence modeling, the paper improves recommendation performance with graph contrastive learning and unsupervised data augmentation. It also introduces an interest extraction and fusion method. Specifically:

(1) Augmented sequences

A generative adversarial network (GAN) generates realistic user-item interaction sequences to enrich the diversity of training data. The generator produces sequences from partial input, the discriminator judges whether a sequence is real, and the two are optimized through adversarial training.

A diversity-promoting term is added to avoid mode collapse and ensure the quality of the generated data.

(2) Building and refining the item relation graph

A global undirected graph is built from all user interaction sequences; nodes are items, and edge weights reflect how often items are adjacent across sequences. The graph structure is refined by defining higher-order adjacency (e.g. connections at different time gaps) and setting thresholds to filter noise. Popular items get fewer edges to mitigate popularity bias.

(3) Graph contrastive learning

Graph contrastive learning is applied to the relation graph: subgraphs are generated by probabilistic neighborhood sampling to make item embeddings more robust. By contrasting positives (augmented representations of the same item) against negatives (other items), item representations are optimized to capture global associations.

(2) Multi-interest extraction and fusion

An improved capsule network combined with target attention captures the user's dynamic and diverse interests. A Transformer encodes the user's interaction sequence, and dynamic routing updates the interest capsules to produce a personalized user preference vector.

The final recommendation score is computed from the interaction between the user preference vector and the target item embedding.

Takeaways: using GNNs for behavior sequences is a good approach: it adds knowledge about the items in the sequence, and I have seen it work in several papers.
