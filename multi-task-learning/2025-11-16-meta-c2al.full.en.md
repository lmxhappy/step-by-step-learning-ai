# Recommendation papers (2025.11.16): Meta uses cohort information as labels to strengthen the network

> [中文版](2025-11-16-meta-c2al.md)

1 C2AL: COHORT-CONTRASTIVE AUXILIARY LEARNING FOR LARGE-SCALE RECOMMENDATION SYSTEMS

Link: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02215

Company: Meta

Idea: cohort information enhancement

Area: network enhancement

Overview:

Cohort information mined through statistics is used as labels that, together with the main label, guide model updates.

(1) Mining cohort information

Statistics are used to mine two representative cohorts, called head and tail.

head (Top 5%): high-density regions, with many samples and common patterns (e.g., highly active users with high CTR and consistent behavior).

tail (Bottom 5%): low-density regions / distribution tails, with few samples and distinctive patterns (e.g., low-activity users with low CTR and diverse behavior).

A given sample may belong to head, may belong to tail, or may belong to neither.

(2) Network change: add two auxiliary task networks, a head network and a tail network, both MLPs, predicting whether the sample is head and whether it is tail. In other words, a shared network outputs an embedding that goes into 3 networks, the main network, the head network and the tail network, which predict the main objective, the head-membership probability and the tail-membership probability respectively. The 3 losses are summed with weights and the gradients are backpropagated to update the networks, including the shared network.

The intermediate output embedding of the main network is fed into two networks, a head network and a tail network, both MLPs, predicting whether the sample is head and whether it is tail. In other words, a shared network outputs an embedding that goes into 3 networks, the main network, the head network and the tail network, which predict the main objective, the head-membership probability and the tail-membership probability respectively. The 3 losses are summed with weights and the gradients are backpropagated to update the networks, including the shared network.

The shared network is based on DHEN, with small modifications.

The cohort information in this paper is built independently along one specific dimension, such as user value, and the dimensions do not cross. Which dimension to choose in the end, the paper tests by enumerating them. How to combine several dimensions together, the paper does not say clearly.

Takeaways:

(1) The usual way to use mined cohort information is as a feature fed into the model. This paper uses it as a target instead.

(2) The cohort information here is not only for users but also for items.

(3) The paper splits cohorts using the distribution and accounting for its differences. You could also consider any other method. Setting that aside, the paper is actually very simple: it uses cohort information as labels to guide the model.

Caveats:

Too theoretical; it demands a lot from the reader and is somewhat hard to read. In fact, the idea is easy to understand.

Credibility: offline

Recommendation: practically useful
